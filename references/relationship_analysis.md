# Analyzing Another Person

Read this reference when the user wants to understand a boss, colleague,
partner, friend, customer, or another participant in a conversation.

## Evidence rules

- Analyze only messages authored by the selected person. Never merge both sides.
- Prefer 50 or more authored messages across more than one day or topic.
- Treat 20-49 messages as directional and fewer than 20 as low confidence.
- Quote short behavioral evidence, but do not reproduce the full conversation.
- Separate observed communication behavior from inferred MBTI preference.
- Account for hierarchy, work roles, culture, stress, and the relationship context.

## Output rules

State the selected person, source, sample count, relationship, and confidence.
Frame results as hypotheses to verify through direct conversation. Give concrete
communication suggestions for the next interaction.

Do not infer protected traits, mental-health conditions, intent, honesty, or
fitness for employment. Do not use the analysis to manipulate, exclude, hire,
fire, or make another high-impact decision.
